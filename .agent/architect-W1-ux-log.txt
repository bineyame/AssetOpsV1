+# W1 UX pass - Architect answers (2026-10-16)
+
+Decision: `D-2026-10-16-w1-ux`. Codex Architect, under `.ai/ROLE_CONFIG.md`. This answers the two questions in `tasks/W1-UX-mockup-fidelity.md` and governs W1-UX-01 through W1-UX-05. It amends the proposed disclosure-only pattern and approves a bounded vocabulary. Owner visual acceptance remains the combined W1 walkthrough.
+
+## 1. Deeper analysis placement - replace the disclosure-only recommendation
+
+The owner's T028 direction and `D-2026-10-04-plain-language-analysis-views` require a separate Analysis destination for substantive method, coverage, comparisons and calculation. Retain the existing Finding, Dispatch and Site Evidence `Overview | Analysis` pattern and the v69 contextual tab hierarchy. For other W1 detail roots, use an existing meaningful Analysis, Evidence, Plan, History, Verification or equivalent detail tab where it owns the deeper material. If a root has substantive technical explanation but no suitable destination, add one contextual **Analysis** tab in its owning W1 slice; do not add an empty tab or duplicate a record trail already owned by Evidence or History. The W1-UX-01 shared component may provide a `View analysis` link with selected Site, period, revision and object context; it must not make a closed disclosure the only route to substantive explanation.
+
+Directory rows, index rows and compact Overview cards do not each need a tab strip. They link to the owning object's detail or Analysis destination. A closed inline **How this was worked out** disclosure is allowed for a few exact references, IDs, versions, raw reasons or support fields on a detail page. It is supplementary; a long table, causal explanation or evidence trail belongs in its contextual destination. Keep exact record links and historical selectors usable. Do not use a separate global expert mode.
+
+At 1440 px, keep the contextual tabs visible under the page heading and link cards to the selected Analysis view. At 390 px, keep the **same named destination** as a keyboard-accessible tab or view link in the normal page flow, with wrapping or horizontal scrolling and an obvious active state; never replace it with a disclosure because the viewport is narrow. A small exact-reference disclosure may remain closed at either width. Material missing evidence, stale/pending/refused state, scope, and consequence limits stay beside the headline at both widths, outside any tab or disclosure. Native disclosure semantics and readable graphic text apply where a small disclosure is used.
+
+## 2. Product vocabulary - accept with tighter boundaries
+
+**Site, Evidence, Finding, Action and Verification** are approved domain labels for navigation and object identity. Explain their meaning in nearby ordinary language on first use in a view: a Finding is a bounded conclusion about an assessed period; an Action is proposed or recorded work; Evidence is the records used; Verification is a measured trial outcome when a result exists. **Analysis** is an approved destination label. Keep canonical route/tab names, including Verification, while a result card can say **Trial outcome**. A label grants no positive claim: `Finding` does not mean confirmed fault, `Action` does not mean applied, and `Verification` does not mean verified. Prefer **What the readings can support** over `claim limit`, **Reporting status** for source contact, and **Battery condition: cannot yet be judged** until a reviewed physical-condition read exists.
+
+Default prose, headings, chart labels, badges, collapsed summaries and accessible names must not expose raw enums or reason codes (`CURRENT`, `UNASSESSED`, `VERIFIED`, etc.), UUIDs, digests, rule/model versions, cursor/watermark mechanics, internal analytics terms (`claim ceiling`, `claim limit`, `basis fingerprint`, `typed reason`) or simulator/development vocabulary (`run`, `binding`, `stage`, `commit`, `fixture`, `payload`) in its implementation sense. Ordinary operational language such as **the generator ran** is fine. Do not render a raw code in the collapsed control's accessible label. Source-owned or user-owned exact names may appear where identity itself is the subject, with a label and explanation; exact IDs and technical codes remain available in labelled fields in Analysis/Evidence or the small detail disclosure. No whole-screen or whole-response vocabulary exemption.
+
+The ban on implementation language also applies inside Analysis and Evidence: technical depth explains the site's operation, evidence and limits, not AssetOps internals. Raw exact references are allowed there as labelled data for traceability. Authored example or generated test data must retain its explicit source qualification; it must not be presented as field equipment readings. W1-UX-01 should apply these rules to rendered API text, graphics, accessibility text and the proposed vocabulary scan.
+
+## Claim order and implementation guard
+
+**Honest state outranks presentation fidelity.** The selected, authorized read owns currentness, zero, amount, source, coverage, outcome and historical status. A headline, graphic or shorter label must not claim more than that read proves. Keep unavailable, refused, pending, stale, withdrawn, unassessed and proved-zero distinct. A fresh receipt is not fresh source facts or current analysis; source contact is not equipment condition; an applied Action is not a verified result; a modelled fuel-cost exposure is not realized loss or savings. Do not copy v69 illustrative numbers into product data. Put material qualifiers next to the answer even when the detailed basis has its own tab.
+
+This is presentation direction only. W1 slices retain their source contracts, exact links, review gates and combined owner walkthrough. No backend or task-packet change is authorized by this decision.
