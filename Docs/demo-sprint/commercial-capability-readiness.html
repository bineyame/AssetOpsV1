1 AssetOps commercial mission and value thesis

Mission (owner, settled). AssetOps helps infrastructure operators systematically improve commercial and operational performance through integrated data, explainable intelligence, actionable decisions, historical accountability and independently verifiable evidence.

Governing principle (owner). Commercial outcomes define the objectives. Physical and economic drivers explain the opportunities. Coordinated capabilities deliver the decisions and interventions. Evidence and verification establish what was achieved. Capability gaps define the work necessary to realize the target system.

The value thesis

Mini-grids, electric bus depots and cold rooms lose money in ways that are physical, recurring and hard to see from a bill or a dashboard. A generator runs when the battery could have carried the load. Solar output is spilled while a paying customer waits for power. A bus leaves the depot short of charge because another vehicle took the shared supply. Stock sits in a warm room because a backup failed to start. Each loss has a physical cause, an operational choice that could change it and a commercial consequence that someone carries.

AssetOps does not earn or save money itself. The operator's intervention does. AssetOps creates value by making those interventions found sooner, chosen better and proven afterwards:

Discover

Find and size opportunities and exposures from evidence the operator can trace to a source reading, with honest unknowns where evidence is missing.

Decide

Compare feasible interventions under the site's physical, contractual and resource constraints, at site and portfolio level, with competing outcomes made explicit.

Assure

Establish independently what changed, technically first and commercially second, so the next decision, the lender and the counterparty rely on evidence rather than assertion.

So the commercial value of AssetOps to a customer is the value of the better interventions it causes, plus the cost of poor interventions it prevents, plus the value of credible evidence (cheaper finance, results-based payments, contract standing). Every claim in the product must say which of these it is and how far it has been proven.

An operational KPI is not a commercial outcome. Fewer generator hours is not lower cost until litres, price and the energy that still had to be served are accounted for. More charger throughput is not more profit if buses then miss departures. Shorter excursions are not saved stock until product quality is known. Every KPI in AssetOps is labelled as either a value KPI or a leading indicator, and a leading indicator never carries a money label.

Example B. EV bus depot: cheaper charging against departure readiness

Two buses share an 80 kW depot supply. B-07 departs at 13:00 and needs 120 kWh into its battery; B-12 departs at 15:00. Equal sharing is cheapest but leaves B-07 9.6 kWh short of its reserve. Giving B-07 priority costs 1.56 USD more in depot energy and meets both departures. An external top-up meets both but costs about 29 USD more and 30 minutes of service time.

The loop applied: observe sessions and connector availability (exists); context from the pinned Foundation limits and declared vehicles (vehicles absent today); evaluate three feasible allocations (target); decide PRIORITY_TARGET; verify later sessions delivered the energy by departure (target EV verifier); reassess cost per departure met; learn which departures repeatedly bind the depot, which feeds the capacity question (EV-5). A charger reaching its kWh never proves the bus ran its trip; fulfilled service needs roster and service records.
