Commercial Capability Architecture

AssetOps, revision 5, 29 October 2026. Architect: Claude (.ai/ROLE_CONFIG.md, "Architect Is Claude By Default"). Branch plan/commercial-capability-assessment; code read at main 504ae5f1. Revision 5 is rebuilt from revision 3 and supersedes revision 4; git keeps both. Owner direction, word for word: .agent/owner-decision-in-delivery-model-2026-10-28.md (all entries, including the 29 October answers). The plan this revision follows: .agent/architect-outcome-iteration-plan-2026-10-29.md.

AssetOps exists to move commercial outcomes for infrastructure operators. This document starts from those outcomes, shows that the product already built is largely arranged around them, and sets out how to organise and steer it the rest of the way: every screen and record labelled by the outcome it serves, every problem and action read as a driver of an outcome, honest indicators where money cannot yet be calculated, and a roadmap derived from the outcomes rather than from features.

The short version. What is built is already well aligned (section 3). The Portfolio already shows per-site state, a needs-attention order, money by kind and progress toward proof. Findings already capture drivers with evidence. Recommendations, Actions and verification already form one complete chain for mini-grid supply cost. The work ahead is mostly organising (labels and links) and extending existing pieces, with a few genuinely new pieces where an outcome has nothing today.

Owner answers, 29 October 2026 (they govern this revision).

  • O1 to O6 are the foundation. The seven outcomes per vertical are instances of them.
  • The Portfolio objective matrix moves to outcome rows.
  • Start with what AssetOps can reasonably determine. Where the money connection cannot be calculated, show directional qualitative indicators (opportunity, or risk toward an outcome) based on drivers.
  • Early stress warnings, and qualitative indicators in general, are accepted until money impact is established. Example: maintenance that happens too often with no result is a revenue leak, and it gets a warning even when the cost per visit is not configured.
  • The old decision-item construct is not used anywhere. The roadmap and the build reference are re-derived from outcomes and drivers. Existing specifications, code and the reviewed, unmerged schema slice are evidence of what exists.
  • What is already built is very aligned; organise and steer it to align even more. "Reimagine" applies to the planning frame, not to rebuilding the product.

How to read the marks. Evidence: IMPL code read and cited as file:line (backend paths relative to backend/assetops_backend/ unless another root is given). PARTIAL some of it exists. TARGET target, not current. ABSENT searched for and not found. Kind of change: ORGANISE a label, link or re-grouping over what exists. EXTEND new behaviour on an existing piece. NEW a genuinely new piece. No test suite was run for this revision (Appendix D).

1 AssetOps commercial mission and value thesis

Mission (owner, settled). AssetOps helps infrastructure operators systematically improve commercial and operational performance through integrated data, explainable intelligence, actionable decisions, historical accountability and independently verifiable evidence.

Governing principle (owner). Commercial outcomes define the objectives. Physical and economic drivers explain the opportunities. Coordinated capabilities deliver the decisions and interventions. Evidence and verification establish what was achieved. Capability gaps define the work necessary to realize the target system.

The value thesis

Mini-grids, electric bus depots and cold rooms lose money in ways that are physical, recurring and hard to see from a bill or a dashboard. A generator runs when the battery could have carried the load. Solar output is spilled while a paying customer waits for power. A bus leaves the depot short of charge because another vehicle took the shared supply. Stock sits in a warm room because a backup failed to start. Each loss has a physical cause, an operational choice that could change it and a commercial consequence that someone carries.

AssetOps does not earn or save money itself. The operator's intervention does. AssetOps creates value by making those interventions found sooner, chosen better and proven afterwards:

Discover

Show each outcome's state, and find the drivers holding it back, from evidence the operator can trace to a source reading, with honest unknowns where evidence is missing.

Steer

Compare levers on those drivers under the site's physical, contractual and resource constraints, with competing outcomes made explicit. People choose and act.

Assure

Establish independently what changed, technically first and commercially second, and read it back against the same outcome.

The commercial value of AssetOps to a customer is the value of the better interventions it causes, plus the cost of poor interventions it prevents, plus the value of credible evidence (cheaper finance, results-based payments, contract standing). Every claim in the product says which of these it is and how far it has been proven.

What AssetOps is, and is not

AssetOps isAssetOps is not
One outcome-management system in which evidence, diagnosis, commercial models, levers, actions and verification share identities and feed each otherA set of dashboards, financial calculators and separate workflows
A system that proposes and qualifies interventions; people choose and actAn autonomous controller that writes to equipment (every integration route is read-only, telemetry integration plan §4)
A source of qualified commercial evidence, each figure labelled by how far it has been proven, and each qualitative indicator labelled as not pricedA P&L, billing or ERP system, or a generator of realized-savings claims from simulation

2 Outcomes, drivers and vertical outcomes

Sections 2.1 to 2.4 are carried from revision 3 unchanged. They are the foundation the rest of the document is organised on. Section 2.5 is new: it makes each vertical outcome an explicit instance of one of the six.

2.1 Six commercial outcomes

These are the organising objectives. They are not six product modules: one intervention usually touches several, and one shared capability serves several. Each outcome names who benefits, the economic mechanism, the KPI that establishes value, and the operational indicators that only lead to it.

An operational KPI is not a commercial outcome. Fewer generator hours is not lower cost until litres, price and the energy that still had to be served are accounted for. More charger throughput is not more profit if buses then miss departures. Shorter excursions are not saved stock until product quality is known. Every KPI in AssetOps is labelled as either a value KPI or a leading indicator, and a leading indicator never carries a money label.

4 The organising model

4.1 The chain

Every part of the product sits on one chain. The chain is the same for every outcome and every vertical. Only the measures, drivers, levers and checks differ.

Where a person's choice sits. Choosing a lever is one step on the chain (the Action). It is where a person exercises authority, and the product supports it with what-ifs and a recommended lever. It is not a planning unit and it does not organise the roadmap.

The value-delivery loop. Revision 3's eleven-stage loop (observe, context, diagnose, quantify, prioritise, evaluate, choose, execute, verify, reassess, learn) remains the system's working view. It maps onto the chain: observe and context feed status; diagnose and quantify produce problems and indicators; prioritise is needs-attention; evaluate is the lever; choose and execute are the Action; verify and reassess are proof; learn updates objectives and expectations.

4.4 Reading an Action back against its outcome

After an Action, the vertical outcome's status is shown before and after, on the same objective, the same basis and comparable windows. Beside it sit the technical verdict and, where one exists, the reassessment. A status change is attributed to the Action only when its verification passed (actions/verification_rules.py:785). Otherwise the screen says "status changed; not attributed". Guardrail outcomes are read back the same way, so a lever that improves cost but harms service shows both.

MG-2 needs attention→Finding: avoidable generator running→Lever: reserve trial→Action applied→VERIFIED, guardrails pass→Reassessment: modelled cost change→MG-2 status again

Today the chain above runs to VERIFIED. The reassessment and the before-and-after status are the missing pieces (Appendix A, "MG-2 proof").

5 Qualitative indicators

Most outcomes cannot carry a money figure yet, because a price, cost or business record is missing. They still have drivers AssetOps can observe. A qualitative indicator says which way a driver points (an opportunity toward an outcome, or a risk to it), on what evidence and how persistently, and stops there. This is how every outcome gets a useful reading before its money exists. It is a first-class part of the model, not a placeholder.

13 Roadmap derived from outcomes

13.1 How priority is derived

The roadmap is a list of outcome steps: the next move for one vertical outcome, or a shared move that serves many. Each is ranked on, in order:

  1. Outcome importance: the H, M, L ranking in sections 7 to 9.
  2. Breadth: how many vertical outcomes and verticals it moves. A step that gives every outcome a status beats one that deepens a single outcome.
  3. Evenness: a step that gives a vertical its first complete chain beats a second chain in a vertical that already has one.
  4. Steering before building: at similar value, ORGANISE before EXTEND before NEW.
  5. Feasibility: open rulings, seams in the code, and validation load on a 16 GB machine.
