# Demo sprint: Africa-first scenario options

**Decision for owner review.** Select **at least three scenarios per vertical**: five mini-grid, three cold-chain and three EV options are below. This is eleven buyer conversations, not eleven end-to-end Friday builds. Tier A is planned as a complete paired simulated replay with accepted evidence; Tier B as a short diagnostic using the same portfolio bootstrap and source drilldown; Tier C as a narrated discovery hook: **“Share your data and we'll show you.”** Tier C has no fabricated amount, count or outcome. Each Tier A story has its own 12–15-minute presentation; Tier B is an optional branch inside that meeting. Country, tariff, equipment, payment and operating model must be confirmed with each prospect.

The [World Bank's Mission 300 FAQ](https://www.worldbank.org/ext/en/energizingafrica/faq) includes mini-grids, productive use and independently verified connections under some results-based financing; [AfDB's productive-use programme](https://www.afdb.org/en/documents/gpn-multinational-productive-use-appliance-financing-programme) and [decentralized-energy investment vehicle](https://www.afdb.org/en/documents/multinational-project-zafiri-dre-equity-platform-project-summary-note) illustrate current appliance and portfolio-capital interest. The [World Bank's Kenya mini-grid case study](https://documents1.worldbank.org/curated/en/792001512392701402/pdf/ESM-cKenyaMiniGridsCaseStudyConfEd-PUBLIC.pdf) documents prepaid/mobile payments. The [IEA documents African electric motorcycle and battery-swap operations](https://www.iea.org/reports/global-ev-outlook-2026/trends-in-other-EV-modes). The [World Bank describes unreliable power as a cold-chain constraint for rural food systems](https://documents1.worldbank.org/curated/en/099053124150533090/pdf/P174321194a3b10131a1ee1c550a837e664.pdf). These sources establish buyer context, not AssetOps savings or financing eligibility.

**Why these, in this order.** M1 answers an immediate commercial utilization decision without inventing collections; C1 identifies a repairable backup failure without declaring spoiled stock; E1 challenges charger availability with delivered energy without inventing rider losses. These three are Tier A. Mini-grid gets two additional choices because remote portfolio O&M and independently verified connections speak to different buyers from M1–M3. The order is a meeting and Friday-delivery priority, not a claim that one African vertical has a larger market than another.

**Scoring.** 1–5 for customer pain/financial significance (25%), analytical differentiation (25%), operating and simulation credibility (20%), implementation reuse/Friday feasibility (20%), and 12–15-minute impact (10%). Weighted total is out of five. Tier also reflects claim support. Effort is incremental to shared setup and conditional, not a promise. Current code evidence and hardware limits are in `readiness-assessment.md`.

**What observations can and cannot carry.** Existing mini-grid, cold and EV packs can simulate PV/load/dispatch, generator state, door/backup/probe events and charger sessions/power sharing. They cannot generate independent mobile-money settlement, customer connection commissioning, a verified RBF count, fuel litres saved, stock disposition or rider income. Public programme interest and buyer economics are context for questions, never inputs to demo arithmetic.

## M1 — productive use of constrained solar headroom

### Why this scenario, for this buyer, in this African context

The buyer is the commercial or operations lead of a rural East or West African solar-hybrid mini-grid selling prepaid electricity, with a mill, pump or cold-room customer whose work *might* shift into daylight. They need more paid use from installed PV while protecting evening service, battery reserve and scarce O&M cash; an investor or DFI will also ask whether the service and revenue story has auditable records. M1 answers the decision they can make now: is there physically usable midday energy worth testing with this customer? It beats M2's diesel-cost pitch for a first meeting because generator kWh cannot establish litres or savings without a fuel curve and refill evidence. It beats M3's collections pitch because meter, vending, tariff and customer joins are absent; an apparent payment gap could be settlement timing rather than leakage.

The proposed buyer-language reveal is: **“How many midday kWh could this mill actually use, and what is their maximum tariff value in our currency?”** Eligible and excluded intervals, feeder/inverter limits, reserve and a declared load make that answer inspectable in minutes 4–7; the gross ceiling is not money collected. Ask in discovery whether diesel deliveries or prepaid settlement hurt more, then say **“we need your fuel/refill or vending exports before we can price or reconcile those.”** A read-only 60–90-day pilot with EMS/feeder data, tariff history and agreed load schedule can test repeatability and, if vending records arrive, whether opportunity converts to paid use. That is a narrower, signable commercial experiment than a promised savings figure.

**World → observation → inference.** A solar-hybrid mini-grid has PV/inverter limits, battery/PCS reserve, diesel backup, feeder meters and uneven demand. A mill or pump customer can move a *declared* load; no automatic control is assumed. The existing mini-grid pack and W4 rules can assess accepted PV, load, storage and dispatch intervals. Show eligible and refused hours, then compare the same weather/demand with the proposed schedule. Low noon output alone cannot prove curtailment: irradiance, CT polarity, battery charging and inverter limits may explain it. Preserve critical service and reserve in both cases.

**Hook and pilot.** At minute 6, the commercial lead sees observed-window eligible kWh and its gross tariff ceiling in local currency. A monthly equivalent is allowed only from enough comparable days, with extrapolation labelled. Prepaid/mobile-money settlement, tariff revisions and customer load flexibility are required to turn potential energy into a collection hypothesis. For an investor/DFI, accepted service/connection records are useful, but AssetOps does not certify results-based financing. Pilot: read-only EMS, feeders, vending/meter exports and tariff history for 60–90 days; agree collection and service thresholds. Remote O&M, diesel logistics and FX-exposed capex remain context, not priced benefits.

**12–15 minutes:** 0–2 portfolio and buyer economics; 2–4 noon mismatch; **4–7 eligible versus excluded day and gross ceiling**; 7–10 source/price drilldown and alternative causes; 10–12 constrained simulated schedule; 12–15 evidence, missing collection data and pilot ask. Fallback: unpriced eligible kWh. Technical challenge: inverter/feeder/battery clock alignment and reserve proof.

## C1 — backup response determines room-air exposure

### Why this scenario, for this buyer, in this African context

The buyer is an East African horticulture exporter/cooperative or dairy cold-store operator paid for accepted produce or storage service, operating a remote room with grid outages and generator backup. Their immediate worry is a shipment or stock hold after an outage, plus a repair trip that fails to fix the cause. C1 ties a missed generator start or transfer to *measured room-air exposure* before discussing stock value. It is stronger than C2's door/loading story for this meeting because the backup-event sequence presents a testable maintenance decision, while door-open heat can be normal handling and needs product-load context. C3 vaccine exposure is more consequential for a health-logistics buyer, but dose disposition requires calibrated, product-specific logging and an authorised quality protocol that this demo does not possess.

The proposed buyer-language reveal is: **“Did our backup fail before the room crossed our temperature limit, and for how long was the air outside it?”** Event order, probe readings, missing intervals and door/defrost alternatives make the minute-5 answer credible to an engineer. Room-air exposure is not spoiled tonnes or a rejected export shipment. For a vaccine prospect, use this as a discovery hook: **“we can investigate the backup chain; we need your validated logger and quality limits before any dose decision.”** A read-only pilot across controller/transfer, calibrated probe, outage and door records, followed by a witnessed generator test, can establish whether the alert and maintenance decision hold on their site. Stock value enters only with the buyer's lot data and approved exposure rule.

**World → observation → inference.** One horticulture export or dairy room, grid/generator, transfer, compressor, door and probe; outage plus failed start at realistic ambient/door conditions. The cold pack and assessments align public events with five-minute probe observations. Compare an otherwise identical simulated backup-response run, including transfer delay. Open door, defrost, warm loading or probe drift can compete; absent backup events make cause unresolved. Air temperature is not product-core temperature.

**Hook and pilot.** At minute 5, the buyer sees how much room-air exposure followed failed backup response and which lot *might* need quality review. Tonnes or local-currency **inventory potentially exposed** require customer lot quantity/value and a signed threshold rule; they are not spoilage, rejected exports or saved sales. A pharma/vaccine prospect gets the causal diagnostic, with disposition reserved for its validated logger and quality team. Pilot: controller/transfer events, calibrated logger, door/outage records and stock/quality rules; witness the next generator test.

**12–15 minutes:** 0–2 facility/shipment; 2–4 backup chain; **4–7 probe excursion, observed gaps and authorised at-risk lot**; 7–10 door/probe alternatives; 10–12 simulated backup test; 12–15 evidence, quality limitation and pilot. Fallback: unpriced bounded duration/degree-minutes and maintenance decision. Technical challenge: event order, logger calibration and no long-gap interpolation.

## E1 — availability hides failed energy delivery

### Why this scenario, for this buyer, in this African context

The buyer is an urban or peri-urban East African e-motorcycle/boda-boda depot or swap operator earning per charge or swap, with riders depending on a small number of chargers and a constrained grid feed. A green connector status does not tell their dispatch lead whether the requested energy arrived before the next dispatch window. E1 wins the first meeting over E2 because an aborted transaction and its metered shortfall are a direct, understandable service failure; E2's shared-feed scheduling question needs credible departure priorities and may become an optimization discussion. E3's depot residual can matter for tariff and diesel spend, but meter boundaries and auxiliary loads must be settled before a residual means anything commercially.

The proposed buyer-language reveal is: **“The plug showed available; how many kWh did this charging request actually get?”** The transaction and meter delta support the minute-5 shortfall, while reconnects, meter epochs and grid derating remain visible challenges. No battery, vehicle or roster join means no count of missed swaps, lost fares or grounded riders. Ask whether weak-grid scheduling or unexplained import spend is their next concern, with **“we need your departure roster or depot-meter boundary before those claims.”** A read-only 60–90-day pilot joins CSMS status, transactions and meters to the depot meter; battery inventory and dispatch windows are added only if the buyer wants a readiness measure. The pilot can measure failed delivery and test a witnessed repair, rather than promise recovered rider income.

**World → observation → inference.** E-motorcycle/boda-boda depot or swap hub with a two-connector charger and constrained 80 kW feed. One session aborts after a fault while another connector remains available. The EV pack and session assessment compare status, transaction events and meter increments. A later simulated cleared-fault run shows what the system could deliver, not field recovery. CSMS restarts, grid derating and meter epoch errors are alternatives.

**Hook and pilot.** At minute 5, reveal the gap between *available connector* and accepted delivered kWh. Count only sessions at risk for a declared dispatch window. Rider earning time, missed fares, stranded minibuses and swap-stock outages need battery/vehicle identity, state of charge and roster evidence. Energy tariff is supported charging spend, not lost revenue. Pilot: read-only CSMS transactions/status/meters plus depot meter and, if readiness matters, battery inventory/vehicle schedule. Ask whether grid interruptions and solar/diesel backup are recorded; no fuel saving without a fuel basis.

**12–15 minutes:** 0–2 fleet/depot economics; 2–4 availability; **4–7 abort versus delivered kWh**; 7–10 events/meter and grid alternatives; 10–12 later simulated witness session; 12–15 source quality, missing dispatch join and pilot. Fallback: delivery failure only, without vehicle or revenue count. Technical challenge: transaction ID and meter continuity across reconnects.

## M2 — diesel dispatch investigation (B)

### Why this scenario, for this buyer, in this African context

A remote solar-hybrid operator faces generator servicing and fuel-delivery costs while trying to use installed PV. **Hook:** “Which diesel runs should my technician investigate first?” Reuse M1's site and accepted generator, PV, load and reserve intervals to show a candidate dispatch mismatch and the source quality behind it. Irradiance, minimum generator loading, reserve policy or maintenance can explain a run; generator kWh does not establish litres, fuel theft, avoided cost or emissions. A pilot needs a customer fuel curve or calibrated flow/refill ledger and comparable dispatch windows before pricing diesel displacement. This is worth a short B diagnostic because it directs scarce O&M attention, not because AssetOps can currently claim fuel savings.

## M3 — prepaid/mobile-money revenue assurance (C)

### Why this scenario, for this buyer, in this African context

A mini-grid CFO or investor may see delivered kWh but still struggle to reconcile prepaid tokens, agent sales and mobile-money settlement. **Hook:** “Can we account for energy delivered, tokens issued and cash settled by customer and tariff period?” The existing simulator can show metered service, but no independent vending, wallet/settlement or customer-to-meter ledger. A difference could be timing, tariff revision, refund, clock or mapping, not theft. Ask for authorised vending/payment exports, refunds, tariff versions and meter/customer mapping. Until those are joined, show an evidence-map question with **no residual amount, collection rate or leakage number**.

## M4 — remote portfolio O&M priority (B)

### Why this scenario, for this buyer, in this African context

An operator with dispersed sites and a small technician team needs to choose tomorrow's visit; a lender wants an explainable service exception rather than a fleet average. **Hook:** “Which site needs our next visit, and what observed event makes it outrank the others?” Reuse the shared portfolio, Site service/dispatch reads and source-health evidence to present one observed exception with its source and time. This is a diagnostic priority, not an automated truck-roll saving or a claim that three fixtures represent a regional fleet. A pilot needs site registry, incidents, work orders, travel constraints and agreed service thresholds. If the existing portfolio read cannot preserve scope and evidence quality, show only the Site exception and keep cross-site priority unranked.

## M5 — connection and investor/RBF evidence (C)

### Why this scenario, for this buyer, in this African context

An expanding mini-grid developer or DFI must report connections and sustained service against a programme's own rules. Some results-based financing schemes require independent verification; service data alone are insufficient. **Hook:** “Which claimed connections can an independent verifier trace to commissioning, customer and service records?” AssetOps can illustrate simulated service lineage, but lacks an independent customer/connection registry, commissioning evidence and funder-specific eligibility rules. Ask for those records, verifier sampling rules and signed metric definitions. Present a pilot evidence map with **no verified household or people count, grant eligibility or investor-grade impact metric**. This decision differs from M4's visit priority and M3's cash settlement.

## C2 — door/loading heat versus equipment fault (B)

### Why this scenario, for this buyer, in this African context

A produce aggregator may dispatch a refrigeration technician for every warm-room alert even when loading practice contributes to heat. **Hook:** “Did the temperature rise while the door was open, or after the compressor failed to respond?” On C1's site, accepted door, compressor and probe observations can support a time-aligned diagnostic. Product loading, defrost and probe position could explain the trace, so no firm compressor diagnosis, spoiled tonnes or recovered sales follows. A short branch is worthwhile only if a clean gap-aware accepted vector exists. A pilot adds loading logs and calibrated probes before changing procedure or maintenance policy.

## C3 — regulated stock excursion (C)

### Why this scenario, for this buyer, in this African context

A health-logistics quality lead needs to know whether an outage calls for quarantine review. **Hook:** “Which lots need quality review after this room-air excursion?” C1 can illustrate backup chronology, but the one-room model cannot certify product-core exposure or dose disposition. Ask for calibrated and validated loggers, lot-to-zone mapping, product-specific limits and the authorised quality procedure. Narrate the investigation handoff with **no dose count, stock loss, release or disposal claim**. A fourth cold scenario would add similar temperature signals without resolving that missing quality evidence.

## E3 — depot meter/session residual (C)

### Why this scenario, for this buyer, in this African context

A depot operator buying grid and backup power may find charger-session kWh below site import. **Hook:** “Which kWh belong to charging, auxiliaries and losses?” EV sessions supply only one side; the independent depot boundary, auxiliary loads, meter epochs and tariffs are not established. Ask for import, backup, auxiliary and CSMS exports plus a metering diagram. Until clocks and boundaries reconcile, give **no leakage, theft or avoided-cost number**. A fourth EV option would need vehicle/route or battery models that do not exist in the current Friday path.

## E2 — constrained feed and charge order (B)

### Why this scenario, for this buyer, in this African context

The buyer is a depot manager serving scheduled e-minibus departures or an e-motorcycle swap fleet where a weak feed or generator backup forces competing charge requests. Their question is which request receives energy before a declared window, not whether a connector is healthy. E2 is more useful than E1 *after* the buyer has accepted delivery measurement: both connectors may work, yet the shared 80 kW cap changes delivered kWh by the deadline. E3's import/session residual cannot answer charge order and needs an agreed auxiliary-load boundary. E2 remains conditional because schedules and vehicle/battery joins are needed to turn kWh into departure readiness.

The proposed buyer-language reveal is: **“With this feed limit, how many kWh reached each request by the declared window?”** A declared request/window and measured power sharing make the energy answer defensible; they do not prove a missed departure, diesel offset or optimal schedule. A read-only pilot would add feeder/backup and CSMS records plus the buyer's departure priorities, then check whether the bounded delivery comparison changes a real scheduling decision. This short branch is worth building only after E1's transaction/meter chain is trusted.

## Shared credibility boundary

Tier A must traverse **private physical state → emulated device observations → durable gateway/receipt → accepted public evidence → domain assessment → qualified financial interpretation or unpriced state → persisted Site/Portfolio view → exact source drilldown**. Private truth is a test oracle only. A paired replay is a simulated comparison; a verified field improvement requires a real accepted post-intervention window. Tier B uses the same accepted bootstrap and source drilldown; missing observations produce an unknown state. Tier C is buyer questions and requested data only. Unsupported mock panels remain honest missing states. See `five-day-plan.md` for dated gates and `readiness-assessment.md` for built status.
