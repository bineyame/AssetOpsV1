# Demo sprint: scenario options

**Selection method.** These are proposed customer stories, not approved requirements. I began with physical operation and likely device observations, then checked what accepted evidence can support. Scores are 1–5 for customer pain (25%), analytical differentiation (25%), physical credibility (20%), five-day reuse (20%), and presentation impact (10%). Total is weighted out of 5. Effort is incremental to the shared story bootstrap, assuming one engineer; it is a range, not a commitment. Current implementation evidence and limitations are in `readiness-assessment.md`.

The three heroes deliberately show distinct value: **mini-grid opportunity**, **EV underdelivery**, **cold-chain exposure and causal response**. M2, E2 and C2 are backups only if the primary observation chain fails or a prospect's data better fits them. Reject M3 and C3 for this sprint because they need unobserved battery or refrigeration condition to make a defensible diagnosis. E3 is useful as a negative-control drilldown, but its commercial decision is less immediate than E1. These scores favor business relevance and analytical distinction, while giving the existing code a meaningful feasibility weight.

## Heroes: physical story → device reports → inference

### M1. Productive load from otherwise unused solar

**Buyer / decision:** mini-grid operations lead or commercial manager deciding whether to move a flexible mill, pumping or refrigeration load to noon. **World:** PV array, inverter AC limit, battery/PCS/reserve, generator and three feeders as in `backend/tests/fixtures/w4/mg-w402-001.yaml`; irradiance and ordinary demand vary across days. Near noon, available PV exceeds current service; the battery is at a protected reserve or charging limit. A declared 10 kW flexible load could use only intervals with supported headroom and no critical-service degradation. The candidate is a schedule change, not automatic control.

**Observed:** timestamped PV, inverter, feeder, battery and diesel reports, plus source quality and tariff assumption. The private simulator computes energy balance and dispatch; device projection may omit or delay a meter; gateway submits sealed public reports; W4 depth infers eligible headroom and W4-03 computes bounded incremental kWh and gross tariff scenario. **Reveal:** a priority opportunity appears only for days where the evidence supports it, with the excluded day and reserve constraint visible. Compare baseline and declared load schedule on the same weather/demand inputs; show scenario estimate separately from a later, accepted measured window. Existing Alternative and Action screens are useful only when populated from that exact basis. A tariff times incremental kWh is potential gross value, **not** revenue or savings; customer must validate load flexibility, tariff, collection and load-side meter. Unobserved curtailment causes or a bad CT polarity can explain the same residual. A demo is misleading if it treats all midday PV potential as saleable energy, counts overlapping periods twice, or hides reduced reserve.

**12–15 minutes:** 0–2 Portfolio and Site context; 2–4 show noon PV/service gap; 4–7 open selected Renewable/Dispatch and accepted measurements; 7–9 inspect headroom/excluded day and money assumptions; 9–11 show bounded load option versus baseline; 11–13 open provenance and later-window status; 13–15 ask for a 60-day read-only pilot with meter topology, tariff and flexible-load schedule. Narration: “The opportunity survives only when service and reserve still work.” Visual moment: eligible and refused days side by side. Technical question: are PV potential, actual output and feeder meters independently available? Commercial question: can demand move and be collected? Fallback: show physical headroom in kWh, leave gross value unpriced if tariff or collection basis is absent.

### E1. Availability is not energy delivery

**Buyer / decision:** depot charging manager deciding whether to repair a connector and change readiness checks. **World:** one charger with two connectors sharing an 80 kW site feed and an enabled charging window, from `simulator/assetops_simulator/packs/ev_charger.py`. A connector authorizes and starts; a plausible fault aborts its session while another connector remains available/charging. After fault clearance, an otherwise comparable later session completes. No vehicle battery or route physics is modeled.

**Observed:** status/fault events, start/abort/end, session meter increments, site import samples and tariff. The device stream does not expose private requested kW or simulated fault forcing. `ev-session/1` and W5 depth establish incomplete versus supported energy; gross tariff applies only to eligible intervals. **Reveal:** an availability tile can appear healthy even when the relevant session delivered little energy; the actual evidence is the transaction and meter chain. Intervention is connector repair/inspection and a witness session. Its later simulated accepted observations may support “delivery restored in this run”; it does **not** prove vehicle readiness, missed route, lost revenue or field savings. A CSMS restart, incomplete meter, bad vehicle association or grid constraint could produce similar readings; show them as alternatives. Without fleet departure/SoC data, phrase the commercial stake as “departure readiness risk” and request the missing feed. Misleading demo: using a declared vehicle ID or simulator label as an observed session join, or pricing the missing kWh as avoided revenue.

**12–15 minutes:** 0–2 Portfolio/depot context; 2–4 Availability versus Sessions contrast; 4–7 open abort and exact event/meter timeline; 7–9 show complete charged kWh, unknown interval and tariff spend; 9–11 show repair decision and later comparable run; 11–13 inspect source IDs/confidence; 13–15 pilot ask: authorized CSMS export plus depot meter, optional fleet schedule/SoC if readiness is to be claimed. Visual moment: “available” connector beside an incomplete charge. Technical question: do session IDs and meter epochs survive CSMS reconnect? Commercial question: what does an undercharged vehicle cost? Fallback: stop at measured short delivery and a diagnostic pilot if fleet join is unavailable.

### C1. Backup chain and exposure, without a spoilage claim

**Buyer / decision:** cold-store operations or quality lead deciding whether to test/repair backup start and transfer. **World:** one chamber, probe, compressor, grid, generator, transfer and bus from `simulator/assetops_simulator/packs/cold.py`; a grid outage occurs at realistic ambient/door load. A forced generator start failure removes compressor power, and room air warms with thermal inertia. A comparable intervention run removes that one failure forcing; the pack's fixed start/transfer delays remain. Do not imply product core temperature equals room air.

**Observed:** grid loss, start request/result, generator/transfer/bus/compressor events and five-minute room probe samples; report timestamp, quality and gaps. The cold assessment aligns the chain and bounds excursion degree-minutes without bridging long gaps. **Reveal:** the room alarm is the end of a causal sequence; a backup response window may be the correct maintenance target. A later simulated run can show narrower observed excursion bounds, but inventory loss and actual savings remain unverified. Financial presentation: current code shows unpriced exposure; a proposed bounded **potential inventory value exposed** requires a customer-authored stock quantity, unit value and explicit exposure rule. Never multiply all stock value by degree-minutes as if it were loss. Other causes include open door, defrost, probe drift and warm product loading; these remain visible. Misleading demo: “failed generator” based only on warm air, “spoilage avoided,” or filling a probe gap with simulator truth.

**12–15 minutes:** 0–2 facility/stock context; 2–4 grid event and backup-chain timeline; 4–7 room trace and bounded excursion with gaps; 7–9 inspect exact events/probe and competing door explanation; 9–11 compare declared backup test with baseline; 11–13 show unpriced or qualified value-at-risk assumption; 13–15 pilot ask: logger/controller/backup events plus inventory policy, calibration and witness test. Visual moment: failed/delayed start preceding the probe excursion. Technical question: where is the probe and which backup events are logged? Commercial question: what inventory and excursion rule does quality approve? Fallback: show bounded exposure and a backup maintenance decision without money if stock data is absent.

## Credibility and minimum execution implications

| Hero | Would this happen; observations a real operator may have | Hidden variable and alternative cause | Smallest credible test and comparison |
|---|---|---|---|
| M1 | PV curtailment and daytime flexible demand are plausible if inverter/battery/service constraints permit; EMS and feeder meters may exist | PV potential calibration, CT sign, actual load flexibility, payment collection | Freeze weather/demand/price basis; vary only proposed load schedule; check kW, kWh, battery reserve and critical service; suppress value if coverage or tariff fails |
| E1 | Charger faults and partial sessions occur; CSMS commonly has connector and transaction observations, depot meter may be separate | Vehicle identity/SoC, grid derating, CSMS missing stop event | Same charger/feed constraints; fault then clear, independent public reports; positive complete and negative incomplete/duplicate sessions; price only supported kWh |
| C1 | Grid outages and backup start failures occur; room logger and controller events may exist but can be incomplete | Product temperature, defrost, door use, probe placement/calibration, inventory | Same ambient/door/thermal parameters; vary the generator-failure forcing only; check heat balance, order, probe gap and bounded duration; no monetary claim without signed stock/rule |

In all three, the required proof chain is **private physical truth (test oracle) → emulated device measurement → durable simulated gateway → authenticated ingestion/accepted evidence → domain assessment → bounded financial interpretation → Site/Portfolio view → exact evidence drawer**. A hypothetical alternative is a counterfactual estimate. A later simulated observation is a replay result, not a verified field outcome. No protocol-wire or physical equipment validation is implied. For pilot acquisition paths and readiness classes see the assessment's hardware table.

The main shared implementation implication is a versioned, idempotent **three-story setup and replay manifest** that creates a plausible three-Site portfolio without manual database edits. Reuse existing Site registration, frozen scenarios, gateway and read models. Add only narrow story producers for the chosen baseline/intervention pairs and a qualified cold value-at-risk assumption if approved. Existing UI is the default; unmerged post-W6 cards improve fidelity but are not prerequisites for truthful hero paths. Use the owner's v6.9 mock for layout and the 25 October UI guidance for local clarity, responsive behavior, focus and empty/error states; do not fabricate populated panels.
