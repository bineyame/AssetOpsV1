# Seven-minute walkthroughs (one per vertical)

Structure from brief Part V: 0–1 opening, 1–2 reveal, 2–4 investigate (with a negative control), 4–6 decide, 6–7 convert. Open `index.html`; every click below names the on-screen label. All figures are illustrative demo data; say so once at the start.

---

## A. Mini-grid: Lakeside Power

**Start:** `index.html` opens on `#/decide-lakeside` (Decision home). Role toggle on **CEO**.

### 0–1 min · Opening insight (script, about 75 seconds)

> "This is Lakeside Power, a demo operator with three village mini-grids. Everything you see is illustrative, built to behave like your records would.
> October's books closed on 6 November. The plan said the three sites would contribute $2,295. They contributed $1,443: $852 short.
> Before we explain why, notice this line: 91.5% of the costs here come from recorded invoices. One line, Kisiwa's maintenance, is still an estimate because the invoice has not arrived. We show that rather than hide it.
> Now the part most people get wrong. Kisiwa burned the most diesel, 2,400 litres. The instinct is to go after Kisiwa's fuel. But every hour Kisiwa's generator ran was needed: it was a rainy month and the evening load is bigger than the solar and battery. The saving is at Kobo, which burned less, 1,180 litres, but 210 litres a month of that is avoidable with one setting change.
> So the first question isn't 'where is diesel highest', it's 'where is diesel a choice'. On the right is what we would do first, within your $5,000 budget and three crew visits."

**Clicks:** none beyond pointing at the three result tiles, the chart and the coverage line.

### 1–2 min · Reveal
1. Point at the two contrast cards ("Highest diesel use is not where the saving is"; "Spare solar is not the same as paying demand").
2. Click **View as CFO**: the question changes to "Why is performance off plan…", the coverage panel moves up; say "same numbers, different emphasis".
3. Click **View as CEO** again.

### 2–4 min · Investigate (negative control: Kisiwa)
1. Click the tab **Why off plan** (`#/bridge-lakeside`). Walk the waterfall: Kobo's hatched "avoidable" step −$262.50 (modelled), the dotted "Fuel not explained yet" −$150, Mto's recorded "Customers not billed after a meter swap" −$56.
2. Click the Kisiwa row **Rainy month: generator needed** → `#/econ-kisiwa`. Show diesel $3,120 recorded, maintenance $700 **Estimated**, fuel balance within tolerance. "This is the negative control: no fuel option qualifies here."
3. Click the site tab **Mto**. Show the energy-to-cash chain: 6,000 kWh delivered → 140 kWh not billed after the meter swap → billed $2,160 → collected $1,890, in transit $150, outstanding $120. "The 120 kWh we cannot explain is not priced: it's an investigation, not theft."
4. Click the underlined **$2,160** → evidence drawer: billing export row layout, the arithmetic, "Emulated source". Press **Escape**.

### 4–6 min · Decide
1. Click site tab **Kobo**, then **Change when Kobo's generator stops** (`#/choice-kobo-generator`).
2. Read the four answer cards. In **Options compared**, contrast: stop setting (+$2,916 over 12 months, three safeguards pass, one costed) vs lower reserve (**Fails**: battery below 35% at 5 am) vs buy storage ($28,000, Later) vs do nothing.
3. Point at "If things change": diesel 20% cheaper still $2,286.
4. Click **Plan and budget** in the left menu (`#/plan-lakeside`). Show $3,880 of $5,000, 3 of 3 crew visits, modelled +$6,096 "not realized", and the three listed-not-added items.
5. Next to the budget, answer **Will the cooperative extend its letter of intent?** Walk the four cases (also in the "All four cases" table):
   - **$5,000 + Yes** (default): claim and wait, spend $3,880, +$6,096.
   - **$5,000 + No**: claim and wait, the huller is lost, spend $2,680, +$2,556; buying now is not affordable ($6,430).
   - **$6,500 + Yes**: claim and wait still wins (+$6,096 against +$3,546 for buying now).
   - **$6,500 + No**: buying now wins, spend $6,430, +$3,546 against +$2,556 (+$990).
   Say: "More budget never lowers the result; whether the cooperative extends is what changes it." Return to **$5,000** and **Yes**.
6. Optional (entitlement): open **Restore Mto's failed inverter** from "Not in the plan" → contract rights panel: Evidence assembled, **21 days left** to the 27 Nov notice deadline, cost to us per path, "This is not a legal decision…".

### 6–7 min · Convert
1. Click **Proof it worked**: three separate answers, all "Not yet", each with the data needed.
2. Click **Pilot request**: seven record categories, what you get, how we check it, the four-week outline. Click **Request the diagnostic** → "Example only: no request was sent."

## B. Cold chain: Harvest Cold Network

**Start:** choose **Harvest Cold Network** in the **Operator** select (top bar) → `#/decide-harvest`.

### 0–1 min · Opening insight (script, about 70 seconds)

> "Harvest Cold Network rents cold-room space by the crate-day. Three walk-in rooms of the same size. October's plan was $1,716 of contribution; actual was $946.
> Here's the comparison that matters. Mwea looks worst: it earns least per crate-day. The usual reaction is 'the compressor must be failing'. But Mwea used 2,650 kWh against 2,635 that a room this empty should use: it is within a fraction of a percent. Mwea's problem is that it's 35% full, not that anything is broken.
> Kirinyaga is the opposite. It's busy, 80% full as planned, yet it used 24% more electricity than its occupancy explains. That one is the equipment.
> And Thika: an outage exposed $2,160 of a customer's tomatoes. Exposed is not lost: inspection confirmed six crates, and we owed $108.
> 97.8% of the costs come from recorded invoices; Thika's $80 maintenance line is an estimate because the invoice is still pending."

### 1–2 min · Reveal
1. Point at the contrast card "The room with the highest cost per crate-day has nothing wrong with it".
2. Click **View as CFO**: the coverage panel shows Thika maintenance as Estimated. Then **View as COO**: "Where the technicians go in November", 2 of 2 visits.

### 2–4 min · Investigate (negative control: Mwea)
1. Click **Why off plan**: Mwea's recorded "Fewer crate-days stored" −$620, and "Less energy at lower occupancy (not a saving)" +$155.
2. Click a Mwea step → `#/econ-mwea`: the expected-vs-actual energy chart and the green verdict "Low occupancy, not equipment".
3. Click site tab **Room C · Kirinyaga**: same chart, red verdict "Equipment, not occupancy" (5,000 vs 4,030 kWh).
4. Click site tab **Room B · Thika**: outage facts and the batch table (one exposed batch, one below its limit, one already checked out). Click **$2,160** → drawer shows it is exposure, not a loss. **Escape**.

### 4–6 min · Decide
1. From Kirinyaga, click **Bring Kirinyaga's energy use back to normal**: repair $350 (+$1,512.40 over 12 months) vs replace $4,800 (−$1,988.40, Later) vs do nothing (−$194 a month).
2. Back to **Room B · Thika** → **Protect Thika's stock until the switch is fixed**: two contract-rights panels: contractor claim **Accepted**, replacement 8 Nov (2 days); insurance notified but not filed because $108 is below the $500 deductible.
3. Click **Plan and budget**: $720 of $2,500; effects listed, not added; replacement and compressor excluded with reasons.

### 6–7 min · Convert
1. **Proof it worked** (Kirinyaga repair; tests marked "Proposed in this mock-up").
2. **Pilot request**: six record categories including the storage ledger and QA notes; **Request the diagnostic** → example toast.

## C. EV depot: Metro Bus Depots (captive fleet)

**Start:** choose **Metro Bus Depots** in the **Operator** select → `#/decide-metro`.

### 0–1 min · Opening insight (script, about 70 seconds)

> "Metro Bus Depots charges its own buses: charging earns nothing, so the measure is cost per bus that leaves on time.
> In October every one of 1,302 required departures left on time. That's the good news. The cost, energy and maintenance at all three depots, was $41,705.12 against a plan of $38,520.80: $3,184.32 over, about $32 per departure. 99.3% of it is from recorded invoices; Kasarani's $300 maintenance is estimated until the invoice arrives.
> Almost all of that is at Embakasi, which charged 55% of its energy at peak prices against a 30% plan, and pushed its monthly peak to 500 kW.
> The obvious fix is 'charge only in the cheapest hours', midnight to five at nine cents. It really is the cheapest bill, $12,975 a month — but only because it buys 9,300 kWh less; per kWh delivered it costs more than the schedule we recommend. We still reject it: in those hours the chargers can't put enough energy in, and three buses would leave short every night.
> Second surprise: Ruiru looks underused, chargers busy 31% of the time, but the grid connection caps it at six more buses, not ten."

### 1–2 min · Reveal
1. Point at the two contrast cards; click **View as CEO** then **View as CCO** ("Demand we can take on": +6 buses recommended, +10 not chosen).

### 2–4 min · Investigate (negative control: Kasarani schedule)
1. **Why off plan**: Embakasi "More charging at peak prices" +$2,604 and "Higher monthly peak demand" +$600 (recorded); Ruiru −$19.68 shown as "Not broken down yet".
2. Click the Embakasi step → `#/econ-embakasi`: peak-share chart, tariff note (assumption until the invoice confirms it). Click **$11,457.60** (peak energy) → invoice lines and charger-log note. **Escape**.
3. Click site tab **Kasarani**: flat tariff, so "Change the charging schedule" saves nothing (negative control); charger 2 fault, 2 missed departures, $240 penalties.

### 4–6 min · Decide
1. Open **Charge Embakasi's buses outside peak hours**: deadline-aware schedule, bill $13,356 a month, +$103,965.60 over 12 months, both safeguards pass; cheapest hours: the lowest bill ($12,975 a month, $381 less) but **Fails** "Every bus leaves with the charge it needs" ("Cheapest bill, but 3 buses leave short every night"); keep current pattern $22,144.80 a month.
2. Open Kasarani → **Keep Kasarani's buses running while charger 2 is repaired**: rights panel (Acknowledged, restore due 9 Nov, **3 days left**); charge one bus at Ruiru on each of the 3 nights until the repair, $90 in total, against waiting (about $240 in penalties and 2 broken departures) and local repair (voids the warranty).
3. **Plan and budget**: $1,590 of $5,000, 0 of 2 technician visits, the order of work. (Only Lakeside has a second budget case.)

### 6–7 min · Convert
1. **Proof it worked** for the Embakasi schedule.
2. **Pilot request**: six record categories (charger log, fleet roster, utility invoice, service tickets, plan, contracts); **Request the diagnostic** → example toast.
