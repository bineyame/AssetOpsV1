How AssetEcon turns evidence into decisions today: the decision-intelligence mechanism as built

This answers sections 1 to 4 of the owner's request of 14 Nov 2026 (owner/owner-decision-intelligence-request-2026-11-14.md). It traces the code from a reading to a Portfolio card, with the mini-grid generator-support Finding as the worked case. How the four Portfolio cards are composed is in convertion_potential.html (rev 3). This document does not repeat that. It covers the decision logic, the lifecycle and what is actually enforced.

The short answer
  1. The chain exists end to end for one mechanism. The generator-support Finding (GENERATOR_SUPPORT_CANDIDATE) has working code at every stage: readings, a dispatch analysis, a Finding, a fuel-cost estimate, a status on the Site board, a trial proposal, a verification verdict and the Portfolio cards. It is not one decision model, though. It is a relay of separate deterministic rules. Each stage gates only on what the stage before it stored, and none of them weighs one outcome against another.
  2. "Moves" does real work. The first outcome a Finding moves (its primary outcome) gets a status signal. An open, unapplied primary Finding turns that outcome to Needs attention, and a later moved outcome turns to Watch (site_outcomes/board.py:692-695, outcomes/status.py:75-83). The primary category also decides some Portfolio counts (chain/claims.py:150-161, estate/portfolio.py:316-319).
  3. "Checks" is presentation and counting only. I searched every reader of the CHECKED role. Each backend reader produces a sentence or a count: "checked here as a safeguard", "checked as safeguards in ACT-…", the "Must not harm" line, the repeat-visit warning's linked outcomes, and two per-category counts. No reader changes whether a Finding is raised, how valid or severe it is, its money value, whether a trial may be proposed, or how a trial is verified. None of the ten CONTRIBUTIONS records has the CHECKED role. Section 2 lists the call sites and the search.
  4. The real reliability protection for this Finding is hard-coded elsewhere and does not read the map. Three things protect reliability. The dispatch rules call a run Necessary when the battery is at or near its reserve or capacity is short (analytics/dispatch_v3.py:290-293). The trial rule needs at least one cited Candidate segment whose lowest usable battery charge is more than 10 points above the reserve. Segments that fail this are ignored, not refused (actions/reserve_trial.py:227-228, :249-256), so a trial can still be proposed when other Candidate segments of the same Finding ran close to the reserve. Trial verification has three guardrails: unserved critical energy, time below reserve and end-of-window battery charge (actions/verification_rules.py:787-800). These match the declared checks on MG-5 and MG-4 by design intent only. Editing the map would change none of them. The mill trial has no guardrails at all (actions/verification_families.py:22, :175, backlog BL-09), even though the map says that opportunity is checked against MG-2 and MG-5.
  5. Case A (a real opportunity) is detected, valued as a gross fuel upper bound, and can be trialled and verified. The pure test suites cover each step (§3.11). The value is 0 to 1.10 × Σ(kW × h × L/kWh) × the declared diesel price. It is not a net cost: it has no battery-wear cost, no value for the PV energy used, and an intervention cost fixed at zero (actions/verification.py:1167).
  6. Case B (the generator is genuinely required) is rejected, not qualified, when the analysis can see the constraint. Battery at or near the reserve, or a capacity shortfall, makes the time Necessary, so no Finding is raised, or an existing one becomes NO_LONGER_SUPPORTED. The battery case also raises an MG-4 early warning. When the constraint is one the analysis cannot see, the run is classified as a full-value Candidate and the checks do nothing. Examples: energy needed for the evening, a spinning-reserve or generator minimum-load rule, a stop-SOC policy, an operator command, or battery energy (kWh) behind the converter's kW rating. The only later safety net is the trial guardrails, which run after a change has already been applied. No test covers this sub-case.
  7. The main gaps between stages. Checks connect to nothing that decides. The fuel value is not used to choose or rank interventions. Authored alternatives and scenarios never change a Finding, its value or a proposal. There is one intervention rule per Finding type, with no competing options and no do-nothing option. No human approval is recorded: every command runs as a SYSTEM demonstration. Actions have no assignee; only work cases and domain actions do. VERIFIED is reachable only on registered-simulation or authored-demonstration evidence (actions/verification_rules.py:708-735).
  8. What VERIFIED establishes is narrow. In one comparable hour after the change, Candidate generator seconds fell by at least 25 % against one hour before it, and all three guardrails held. Money is credited only when that result is VERIFIED, its cost check is a modelled improvement, and a priced total is stored (outcomes/credit.py:57-72). Even then the label says it is modelled, not a realised saving.

1.2 Where the stages are not actually connected

  1. Outcome map → detection, validity, valuation, eligibility, verification. None of these stages reads the map. The dispatch analysis, the episode matcher, the fuel model, the trial rule and the verifier take no outcome key at all. Search in §2.2.
  2. Fuel consequence → choice of intervention. The trial rule's inputs are lifecycle, currentness, SOC and reserve (actions/reserve_trial.py:105-118). The money is never an input, so a large and a tiny fuel exposure get the same proposal.
  3. Authored alternatives and scenarios → the Finding or the proposal. mini_decisions/alternatives.py:12-13: "The W1 Finding, its revisions, claim and money are only read". A RULED_OUT or PLAUSIBLE alternative explanation changes nothing downstream. Scenarios are stored what-ifs. No Action is raised from them.
  4. Verification guardrails ↔ checked outcomes. The guardrails are hard-coded (verification_rules.py:787-800). The mill family has guardrails: {} (verification_families.py:175).
  5. Intervention cost → anything. The cost check always uses InterventionCost(0, None, "Generator stop setting changed; no direct cost declared") (actions/verification.py:51, :1167).
  6. Real-source verification. source_pair passes only REGISTERED_SIMULATION or AUTHORED_DEMONSTRATION (verification_rules.py:688, :718). Any other source is UNKNOWN → INCONCLUSIVE "SOURCE_NOT_COHERENT" (:769-771).
  7. Human approval and assignment. Every Action command runs as SYSTEM_DEMO, "no operator approval is recorded" (actions/lifecycle.py:53-58). The action table has no owner or assignee column. Assignment exists only on work cases and domain actions (§4).
  8. Battery wear, PV value, and outage cost → value. Not modelled. The battery warning's price line says "Needs a tested battery wear model" (outcomes/warnings.py:45). MG-5 has no money record ("MG-5 has none (R9)", outcome_map.py:149).

2. What "moves" and "checks" actually do

The honest answer. The map is a lookup table: "FINDING:GENERATOR_SUPPORT_CANDIDATE": (("MG-2",), ("MG-5", "MG-4")) (outcome_map.py:19). Moves drives status: the primary moved outcome turns Needs attention while the Finding is open and unapplied. It also feeds two Portfolio counts. Checks drives words and tallies. The Finding is listed again under Protect with "· checked here as a safeguard". A verified Action gets an "Earlier" line. A "Must not harm" sentence appears once a verification plan exists. Two category counts include it. Nothing that decides reads it. The reliability safeguards that do exist for this Finding are separate hard-coded rules that happen to measure the same things as MG-5 and MG-4.

It does not hold for the mill (BL-09).

Code does. moves[0] is primary (outcome_map.py:76). The board marks it with primary = (i == 0) (board.py:694-695). The order is fixed in ENTRIES, never computed. 2.4 The ten questions

Q1. What does "moves" actually do?

It turns the primary outcome to NEEDS_ATTENTION while the Finding is open and its Action is not yet applied (status.py:75-76). After the change is applied, or for a secondary moved outcome, it gives WATCH (:81-83). It keeps the outcome UNKNOWN while Findings are unsettled (board.py:429-433). It places the Finding's title, headline and thread line on its row, and its first category feeds the Portfolio counts (chain/claims.py:150-161, estate/portfolio.py:316-319). Tests: test_out_b1_status.py::test_rule4_open_primary_finding_without_applied_action, test_out_b1_board.py::test_kobo_mock_grid_and_issue_order.

Q2. What does "checks" actually do?

It produces three sentences: the safeguard issue line, the Earlier line, and "Must not harm" once a verification plan exists. It also adds to two Portfolio tallies and the repeat-visit warning's linked list (table 2.2). Test: test_out_b1_board.py::test_kobo_mock_grid_and_issue_order. With F-104 open, Optimize is NEEDS_ATTENTION while Protect stays NO_OBJECTIVE and only lists the Finding with "· checked here as a safeguard". Also test_mini_protect_battery_outcome_measured_without_warning: MG-5 and MG-4 read NO_OBJECTIVE with the Finding open.

Q3. How are primary and secondary outcomes determined?

By position in the declared tuple (§2.3). Nothing is learned or computed.

Q4. Does a checked outcome affect finding generation, validity, severity, valuation, recommendation, action eligibility, or only presentation?

Only presentation and counts. Generation: dispatch_v3.py and finding_episode.py import nothing from outcomes. Validity: lifecycle depends only on the classification of segments (finding_episode.py:583-621). Severity: the only severity-like value is confidence, which comes from margin and transition (dispatch_v3.py:361-377). Valuation: fuel_cost.quantify takes no outcome. Recommendation and eligibility: reserve_trial.RuleInputs has no outcome field (reserve_trial.py:105-118). Verification: the guardrails are literals (verification_rules.py:787-800).

Q5. Can a negative check veto, constrain, qualify or downgrade an opportunity?

Through the map: no. A bad MG-5 or MG-4 state, whether a missed objective or a battery warning, sits next to the Finding without touching it (status.py:55 evaluates each outcome on its own signals: mine = [s for s in signals if s.outcome == outcome.key …], :73).

Through hard-coded rules: yes, at three points, none of them tied to MG-5 or MG-4 keys.

  1. Before a Finding exists. Reserve or capacity constraints make the time NECESSARY (dispatch_v3.py:290-293). That is a veto: no Finding is raised.
  2. Before a trial. The proposal is refused only when no cited Candidate segment has its lowest usable SOC above r + 10 (reserve_trial.py:219-229, :249-252). The reason returned is then the last segment's reason (verdicts[-1]["reason"], :251-252). That is often SOC_NOT_ABOVE_HEADROOM, but not guaranteed. One eligible segment is enough, and the others are ignored. test_s6_action_rule.py::TestUnavailableReasons::test_any_eligible_segment_is_enough_and_is_cited pins this with an ineligible segment that has no readings. A segment that is ineligible because it sits inside the headroom is ignored by the same code path (:249-250), but no test pins that case ( inputs from actions/adapters/postgres_action_store.py:159-215). This is a weak veto. Candidate segments reached through rule 3 (PV alone) never check SOC, so a Finding whose other segments sat inside the headroom can still be offered the trial.
  3. After a trial. A measured guardrail failure resolves the trial INEFFECTIVE with GUARDRAIL_FAIL (verification_rules.py:807-809) and blocks new trials on that battery (actions/service.py:60-68, :159-166). The Finding, its value and MG-2's state are not downgraded. The thread goes back to REOPENED, the same as a plain target miss (chain/lifecycle.py:51; lifecycle_run.py:50-55 passes only VERIFIED or INEFFECTIVE).

Tests: test_s6_verification_verdict.py::test_measured_guardrail_failure_is_ineffective_with_guardrail_fail (pure, run). test_s6_verification_evaluator_postgres.py::TestEvaluator…::test_guardrail_failure_closes_but_blocks_a_different_finding (PostgreSQL, not run). The unserved-critical-energy FAIL branch on its own (:788) is not covered by tests. Only its UNKNOWN branch is (test_missing_critical_role_and_unknown_target_and_blockers_are_inconclusive).

Q6. How are conflicting outcomes handled?

They are not reconciled. Each outcome is evaluated on its own (status.py:55-102). A row takes the highest-precedence state among its outcomes (:105-113). Rows "are never combined into a score" (:4-6). Probe example: a generator run whose first half is Candidate and whose second half has the battery near its reserve. It yields a Finding over the Candidate half (MG-2 Needs attention, with money) and a "Battery at or near its reserve" warning (MG-4 Watch, outcomes/warnings.py:253-260, site_outcomes/mini.py:1189-1197) at the same time. Nothing links them. The mixed interval itself is not covered as one test.

Q7. Are checks evaluated using measurements, declared objectives, rules, models or status classes?

The map's checks are not evaluated at all. Separately, MG-5's own status uses a measurement against a declared objective: unserved critical kWh on the board day (mini.py:835-858, objectives.py:63-65). MG-4 has no declarable measure and gets status only from warnings: battery near reserve and plan due (status.py:77-78, :84-85). The trial guardrails are deterministic rules over measured five-minute slots (verification_rules.py:392-560, :787-800).

Q8. What happens when a checked outcome has insufficient evidence?

On the board, MG-5 reads UNKNOWN or "not judged" (mini.py:846-855, status.py:94-97), and nothing reaches the Finding or MG-2. The trial proposal does not look at MG-5 evidence at all. In verification, any UNKNOWN guardrail makes the verdict INCONCLUSIVE (verification_rules.py:801, :805-806). A Site without critical-load roles bound can therefore never reach VERIFIED (test_s6_verification_rules.py::test_missing_critical_binding_unknown, test_s6_verification_verdict.py::test_missing_critical_role_and_unknown_target_and_blockers_are_inconclusive).

Q9. Are checked outcomes evaluated independently or as part of one integrated assessment?

On the board, independently, one outcome at a time. In trial verification, as one integrated verdict over the target, three guardrails, comparability bands and source coherence (verification_rules.py:739-815). That verdict is not connected to detection or valuation, and it is not keyed by outcome.

Q10. What happens when an intervention improves one outcome but degrades another?

Reserve trial: if critical energy goes unserved, SOC drops below the new reserve, or the window ends with less charge, the verdict is INEFFECTIVE GUARDRAIL_FAIL. No money is credited (credit.py:64), and the battery is blocked from new trials. The trade-off is never priced: there is no value for MG-5 or MG-4. Mill: nothing happens. Its verdict looks only at midday kWh (verification_families.py:297-347). Board: the two rows simply show their own states.

3.10 Proposed intervention, verification, and what VERIFIED establishes

Proposal (reserve_trial.py): exactly one change, reserve r → r − 5 percentage points, for 15 ≤ r ≤ 90. Verification (verification.py, verification_rules.py):

  • Freezing a plan needs an APPLIED Action with a committed change. The baseline hour must end before the change takes effect, the later hour must start after it, and the baseline must hold positive Candidate seconds (verification.py:222-266).
  • The target is 4 × later_candidate_seconds ≤ 3 × baseline_candidate_seconds (verification_rules.py:675-682).
  • The guardrails: unserved critical kWh may not rise; seconds below the reserve must be 0 (five-minute sampled, "not continuous physical SOC proof"); end-of-window SOC may not fall (:787-800).
  • Comparability: feeder and PV energy within 10 %, initial SOC within 2 points (:36-41, :585-609). Source: both windows simulated or authored (:708-735).

What VERIFIED establishes: on one comparable pair of hours from labelled simulated or authored evidence, the Candidate generator time fell by at least 25 % and the three guardrails held. The resolution itself says "no causal or savings claim. RESOLVED can later be withdrawn" (actions/lifecycle.py:96-98). 5.7 Reusable across verticals, and what the structures can support

  • Reusable today: the outcome map, the status rules and roll-up, the strategy registry, the thread lifecycle (one subject mapping row per domain, chain/threads.py:30-31), the claims and the never-summed rules.   • Cross-Site comparison: per-Site rows, states and claims are returned side by side (site_outcomes/read.py:718-737).   • Shared economic drivers: the declared prices (diesel, productive tariff, EV tariff) are per Site (fuel_cost.py:440-474, the window price per Site). Nothing links two Sites to one driver, and no sensitivity runs across Sites.
  • Portfolio prioritisation: not supported. 8.3 The simulator

  • Mini-grid. packs/minigrid.py:1-44 covers PV, the battery and loads: linear PV with no temperature derate, inverter clipping, a fixed 95 % efficiency, a controller that requests battery power, and a declared priority cascade in exact Fractions. packs/mini_grid.py:1-35 adds the generator. Its lifecycle is STOPPED → STARTING → RUNNING → STOPPED. FAILED is "inert", and runs can start only from STOPPED or FAILED (:8-13). RUN is requested at or below the start SOC, and STOP only after the minimum runtime and at or above the stop SOC (:14-18). "The rating is a ceiling, never a command" (:21-23), and the generator charges the battery only when the battery is not discharging (:721-730). Fuel is "linear: no idle fuel and no start fuel" (:24-27, :86-90). Model v5, used by the demo through v6, starts the generator at min(start, reserve + 5) (:1141-1156). A scenario can override the stop threshold (:949-961, used by kobo-mini-grid-stop-60.yaml, which is not on the story calendar).
  • Cold (packs/cold.py:1-19, cold-thermal/1). One chamber, probe, compressor, grid supply, and a backup generator behind a transfer switch. One exact step per UTC minute: heat leak and door heat against compressor cooling, divided by thermal capacity. Nothing is clamped. The generator start delay (2 min) and transfer delay (1 min) are pack constants, not Site properties (:33-34). Its observation transform has zero bias, 0.1 °C rounding and +3 s latency, and can DROP, HOLD or ADD reports (observation/transform.py:343-348).
  • EV (packs/ev_charger.py:1-20, ev-charger/1). Two connectors under one charger share the Site import limit. Per minute it allocates max(0, site_limit − base_load) in 1 kW round-robin quanta, zero while the grid is unavailable, driven by authored PLUG / AUTHORIZE / REQUEST_KW / FAULT / CLEAR / UNPLUG forcing. The registered model is ruled to TUESDAY 08:00-16:00 only (host/ev_publication.py:282, :331; tools/demo_story_days.py:49).

8.7 What this means for Case A and Case B (§3.11)

  • Case A is clean by construction. On the overuse days (S-14, S-7) and the trial-before day (S), all under the 30 % reserve, the battery dips below the 35 % start threshold before sunrise and the 90 % stop policy then keeps the generator on. Meanwhile 800 W/m² gives about 48 kW of PV available against 15 kW of load (scenario purpose, kobo-mini-grid-reserve-trial.yaml:23-24). By the §3.3 rules that is rule 3, PV alone. I inferred this from the scenario values and did not run it. The demo therefore exercises the PV-alone Candidate path, which no dispatch-3.2 unit test pins (§3.11).
  • The trial's success is partly built into the simulator. In model v5 and later, lowering the reserve from 30 % to 25 % also lowers the start threshold from 35 % to 30 % (mini_grid.py:1141-1156). The pre-dawn dip no longer crosses it, so the generator never starts (scenario :24). The verification then measures the effect the model was written to produce.
  • Case B is clean by construction too. Case B appears on only two calendar days, S-28 (normal) and S-21 (overcast). On those days the battery starts at 40 % (reserve + 10) and holds at its reserve. PV is at most 6 kW while 42 kW is demanded (kobo-s3-dispatch-necessary-run.yaml:1-13). Rule 1 (B1, near or at the reserve) fires before rule 2 (B2, capacity short), and either makes the time NECESSARY. That is why the battery early warning appears on those days.
  • B4, the false Finding, never appears in the demo. The simulator has no evening-energy logic, no spinning reserve, no generator minimum load and no operator command on the calendar, so none of these can cause a generator run (table above). Two B4 items, a SOC dip between reports and battery energy (kWh) behind the PCS kW rating, could arise in principle, because the simulator models battery energy. But no calendar scenario is built to produce them. The stop-SOC policy is the deliberate cause of Case A in the demo. It would become B4 only if a Site kept it for a reliability reason, which the simulator cannot express. The weakness §3 identified is real but unexercised: the demo cannot show it, and it cannot show that it is absent.

How cost scales with days and Sites

  • With days, one Site: faster than linear (measured). Runs of the same scenario get slower as the Site accumulates history. The fitted slope against earlier runs is 0.21–0.49 s per earlier run, and every run kind rises:
    • steady-before: 18.2 → 34.8 s over 12 runs
    • before-rest: 14.0 → 21.2 s
    • night-early: 11.0 → 29.6 s
    • steady: 36.8 → 45.5 s

  • Model (derived). Per Kobo-like Site-day, the child cost is roughly c(d) ≈ 33 s + 2.0 s × d, where d is the days already built. This is fitted so that the 28-day mean is 62 s and the slope is the per-run slope × 2.6 runs per day × 2.6 runs per day of history. To that add the per-day downstream work, ≈ 26 s for a Kobo-like day:
    • mini-depth ≈ 11 s (2,996 jobs over 38 mini-grid Site-days × 0.14 s)
    • chain snapshots ≈ 10 s (825 Kobo snapshots × 351 ms ÷ 28)
    • day and period rows ≈ 5 s
  • With Sites: linear, because Sites build one after another (tools/demo_combined.py:1080-1088). Each tick visits the Sites in order (backend/assetops_backend/scheduling/tick.py:382). Conclusions for the owner.

  1. Simulating a Site-day costs well under a second. Building one costs about 1–1.5 minutes today, and the cost per day rises with the Site's history.
  2. The largest levers are structural, not the simulator:
    • the O(D²) re-read in matching;
    • Sites built in series;
    • about 1 s of process start-up per idle stage per Site per tick;
    • a 25-minute self-check.
  3. Portfolio-scale history (tens of Sites × a year) is not feasible with the current build path without parallel Site builds and per-Site incremental regeneration.
