0 Summary

The recommendation in one paragraph

Do not wait for a customer export. No public dataset contains a whole African mini-grid, bus depot or cold room, so the demo should be built on a "real inputs, AssetOps physics, labelled origin" pattern. Real public measurements supply the things nobody can invent credibly: solar resource, ambient temperature, measured load shapes, charging-session behaviour, duty cycles, door-event responses and published tariffs. The existing simulator packs, extended where needed, turn those inputs into physically consistent telemetry. That telemetry is published through the same gateway and ingestion path the product already uses, and every series carries an origin label. Raw public readings are also replayed through the merged CSV collector, the Modbus emulator and the BeagleBone. That proves the pipe takes real-world signal shapes, but it carries no money.

Six things this plan changes or establishes

  1. One origin vocabulary. Every series carries one of six origins: observed, derived, modelled, declared, synthetic or fault-injected (§1). The vocabulary lives in a dataset manifest, not in the product schema. It reaches the screen through the existing basis and claim-record copy.
  2. A distinct source label, no new money class. Reconstructed histories are published as REGISTERED_SIMULATION where a single registered run can carry them, or as AUTHORED_DEMONSTRATION under a fixture spec that names its reference basis. When the run or fixture is pinned to a registered dataset pack, the server adds a reference-inputs attribute and the screen reads "Simulated with real public inputs" (owner OD-2). Money rules are those of the underlying class. Neither claims field reality. Raw replay through the field path gets no publication and reads UNKNOWN, as TEL-05 and TEL-06 already assert (§1.3, §7).
  3. Three engine extensions are on the critical path. A series forcing input for the mini-grid pack, an ambient series for the cold pack and a many-session EV pack. They are needed because the current scenario contracts cannot carry 13 weeks of real inputs (§3.4, code facts in §11).
  4. 91-day (13-week) histories. The run limit becomes a step budget sized by measurement, and the offset limit no longer caps a scenario at one week (owner OD-4, card DD-05a). Seven baseline weeks, the first three free of injected faults, then six post-intervention weeks (§4).
  5. TEL-10 is re-timed, not cancelled. It stays the only route to an R3 vendor claim and moves to the first pilot export. TEL-06 is reused unchanged with new profiles (§7).
  6. A sequenced backlog of 15 tasks (DD-00 to DD-13, plus DD-05a). It starts in Lane T after TEL-07 and in Lane C after CC-D1, and does not touch either lane's open files (§8).

1 Origin labels and provenance

1.1 Six origins, one per series

The owner asks that observed, derived, modelled and fault-injected data are always told apart (§1, §4). Two more origins are needed to describe the demo honestly: declared inputs, such as asset ratings, tariffs and lot values, and synthetic series built by statistical resampling. Each signal in each dataset pack carries exactly one origin per interval.

1.3 Provenance: what a public dataset needs to be labelled honestly

FACT The resolver knows two positive classes, AUTHORED_DEMONSTRATION and REGISTERED_SIMULATION (evidence/source_provenance.py:40-44). Anything unbound is UNKNOWN, and a field-path batch has no publication (.agent/telemetry-rulings-2026-10-27.md, D1 and D5(d)). 2 Dataset inventory and selection matrix

Every source the owner named was checked again, independently, on 28 October 2026, and alternatives were searched with a preference for African, downloadable data. No large file was downloaded. Small samples went only to the scratchpad. "Selected" means a role in the plan; "template" means the source is used only for statistics or shape and is not redistributed; "rejected" gives the reason.

Headline finding

FACT No public, downloadable dataset was found that holds measured operations for a whole African mini-grid (PV, battery SoC, genset and customer metering together), an African EV depot (sessions, SoC, faults, tariff together) or an African walk-in cold room (temperature, compressor, door, energy together). Measured African mini-grid data exists but is held privately: Renewvia, and the CrossBoundary / Energy 4 Impact Mini-Grid Innovation Lab, with 550 million data points from 62 sites. This is why the plan reconstructs rather than replays.

6.4 Counterfactuals and no double counting

Each intervention is evaluated against a twin run: the same manifest, the same seed and the same real inputs, with only the named intervention removed. The twin is never published as evidence. Can a public real dataset be replayed through the generic CSV collector or the Modbus emulator?

CSV collector: yes, for interval signals

FACT The collector validates a whole file against a profile and admits each cell as one report; it does not resample (export_csv.py:1-22). So a public series can be replayed after preparation: UTC Z stamps at interval end, rows a whole multiple of the span from midnight, exact multiples of output_resolution, blank cells for gaps.

It needs a new profile whose rows the reference Site binds, for example generic-interval-csv-reference-1 with a 3600 s span. It cannot carry battery SoC or other state signals (TEL-06-Q1) or events.

Modbus emulator: yes, with a small generator

FACT The emulator serves pinned JSONL of public observations, each interval mean held constant across its span (rulings, amendment for TEL-05b, item 2). A generator that writes the same JSONL shape from a reference series is a small, contained task. It can serve state signals too.

The register block stays the AssetOps illustrative block, and the BeagleBone can run it at rate 12, as TEL-08 does.

What either proves

R2 for the interface only: "values from <public dataset>, time-shifted, through an illustrative format or register block". Provenance is UNKNOWN and money is withheld, which TEL-05 and TEL-06b already assert.

Only observed series should be replayed this way: irradiance, measured loads and PATH ambient. Replaying a modelled series (PVGIS P) through the field path would dress a model up as a reading.

10 Owner decisions

All eight were answered on 2026-10-28 (.agent/owner-answers-demo-data-2026-10-28.md). OD-1, OD-3, OD-6 and OD-8 are accepted as recommended. OD-2, OD-4, OD-5 and OD-7 are revised below, each with the owner's words first.

OD-1 · Adopt "real inputs, AssetOps physics, labelled origin" as the demo standard

Example: the Turkana site's weather is NASA POWER's measured weather for September 2025, shifted to 2026; its battery SoC is computed by our model and labelled "simulated".

Recommendation: adopt. Rationale: it meets the owner's real-data-first principle with what actually exists, avoids waiting for an export, and keeps every claim at the honest rung.

Decided 2026-10-28: adopted.

OD-2 · How reference-grounded histories are labelled

Owner, 2026-10-28: "it is important to preserve proper source labels, it could share the money rules".

Example: a generator-cost Finding at MG-REF-TK1 carries the label "Simulated with real public inputs", distinct from the plain "Simulated" of KOBO-001, and its money is computed exactly as KOBO-001's would be.

Decision (revised): option A of §1.3. The label is a server-derived reference_inputs attribute of the existing positive class, present only when the run or fixture is pinned to a registered dataset-pack manifest. The money gate reads the class only, so the money rules are shared by construction. Cost: about 0.5 builder-day, folded into DD-12 (now 1.0 d). Rejected: a new positive class (option B), at 3 to 4 builder-days with three CHECK migrations and an audit recapture, which would buy the same label with a weaker proof.

OD-3 · Site naming

Example: "Turkana hybrid (reference)", not the name of the operator whose public site size it resembles.

Recommendation: place and type names only, with "reference" in the display name. Rationale: naming a real operator would imply their data, which the demo does not have.

Decided 2026-10-28: accepted.

OD-4 · History length

Owner, 2026-10-28: "current run limits should not impose unrealistic constraint on possible real world reference. I don't see why a 90 should not work".

Example: the Turkana history runs from 27 July to 26 October 2026: three clean weeks, four weeks with the reserve fault, the intervention on 14 September, then six weeks after it.

Decision (revised): 91 days (13 whole weeks, so weekdays line up). What each limit protects, and what changes: the 100,000-minute run limit protects the worker from a run that cannot finish, but counts minutes rather than steps, so it is replaced by a step budget sized by measurement, with a 366-day calendar bound kept. The 10,000-minute offset limit catches typos, but would have blocked faults placed after the first week even at 56 days; it now follows the run bound, and an entry outside its run is refused at run creation. The 200-entry timeline limit keeps a scenario readable on screen; it stays, because real series travel as one pinned reference. All of this is DD-05a, which measures memory and time on this 16 GB laptop before any producer runs (§5.3). Fallback: if one vertical cannot meet the budget even with streaming, it alone stays at 56 days and says so.

OD-5 · Licence posture

Example: the NREL load workbook (CC-BY) is excerpted into the repository with attribution; Caltech ACN sessions are not, and only fitted dwell and energy quantiles are committed.

Owner, 2026-10-28: "this is for test and demo purposes, not production, consider reasonable license aspects in that context".

Decision (revised): the test-and-demo posture of §5.1: use any publicly downloadable source, always with attribution; commit open-licence excerpts and every source's fitted statistics; keep raw files from sources without an open licence in an ignored local cache; never hand raw restricted data to a customer. Still real: no non-commercial sources (a sales demo is commercial use), no shared registration tokens, no operator names, and a re-read of the terms at the first pilot or production use.

OD-6 · A third, data lane

Example: a Lane D builder takes DD-05 to DD-10 in the next sprint while Lanes T and C finish their milestones.

Recommendation: open Lane D at the start of the next sprint, not before 3 November. Rationale: the engine tasks need full suites, and the heavy-job queue is committed until CC-D1 merges. The light tasks fit Lane T's gap after TEL-07.

Decided 2026-10-28: accepted.

OD-7 · Ask for restricted data in parallel (withdrawn)

Owner, 2026-10-28: "I don't understand the goal here, why would sending a request unlock access?"

Decision (revised): withdrawn. The owner is right that a request does not unlock access. The data sits behind research or data-sharing agreements: Renewvia's with named partners, the Innovation Lab's under each developer's confidentiality terms. A request can at best open a conversation that leads, over weeks or months, to an agreement with use limits. That cannot land in the demo timeline and would likely exclude a sales demo anyway (§9). Renewvia is approached as a pilot prospect, where data comes under the pilot agreement; the Lab's public reports remain calibration sources.

OD-8 · EV focus

Example: the depot is buses (matching D3 and the commercial architecture); the e-boda hub with real Nairobi duty data is optional.

Recommendation: buses first; e-boda only if a pilot prospect is a two-wheeler operator. Rationale: the commercial outcomes and D3 are written for buses; e-boda would need its own outcome set.

Decided 2026-10-28: buses first.
