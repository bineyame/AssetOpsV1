# Owner: long-page organisation and the missing design philosophy (2026-11-20)

The owner asked:

> what is your view on how information is organized on the decions page and other pages where data spans across multiple screen scrolls

The coordinator's view: the first screen of the Portfolio and the new Decision page is right (answer and action in about 5 seconds), but below the fold the pages have no structure:
1. The Decision page's "Why we recommend it" is a junk drawer: reasoning, what would change it, every option in detail, limits, cover, who pays, capacity, verification and the old process grid in one collapsible; collapsed it says nothing, expanded it is several screens with no navigation.
2. Section headings name a topic, not an answer ("Cover under each option", "Limits"); a closed section should still be useful ("Limits: every option passes"; "Who pays: we pay $1,200; the warranty does not cover it").
3. Context and action scroll away: three screens down there is no title, key number or primary action; top products keep a slim sticky header with them.
4. No wayfinding on long pages (Site, plan): no in-page section menu or sub-tabs with the current place shown (phone already has the Section menu).
5. Long lists and tables render in full; the guide's at-most-7-rows-then-"Show all", detail-in-a-drawer rule is applied only on the Portfolio.

Proposed long-page standard: (1) the answer and the primary action first; (2) a sticky header after scroll with title, key number, state and primary action; (3) sections titled as questions with their answer visible when closed (for the Decision page: Why we recommend it; What it changes; Limits and cover; Cost and who pays; How we will check it worked); (4) in-page navigation for any page over about 2½ screens; (5) lists capped at 7 rows with "Show all", row detail in a drawer; (6) a gate failing a page over the budget without section navigation, or a section heading without an answer line.

The owner replied, verbatim:

> yes, send it to the architect and let it address the overall underlying design philosophy that is missing
