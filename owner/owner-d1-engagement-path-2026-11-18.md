## Coordinator's answer (coordinator's analysis; the owner approved it, see below)

The owner's path is sound and, for a new product, better than D1 as written. D1 as written makes the founder the bottleneck (a diagnostic is about 11–19 owner-days and selling largely stops while one runs), front-loads hand analysis (transcribing records), and asks a new vendor to charge for analysis before trust exists. The owner's path is how operators buy software, surfaces real blockers (data access, privacy, process ownership) early, puts the work into the product (the integration pack maps onto the canonical data contract and source adapters) and leads straight to SaaS.

Two things kept from D1 as safeguards:
1. Value on the operator's own data before integrations finish: during the deep-dive take file exports (billing, fuel, plan) through the same canonical contract and show their first plan-against-actual breakdown in the product within a few weeks; it becomes the pilot's baseline.
2. A form of commitment: a pilot fee, modest or credited against the first SaaS year; price disclosed after the client conversation (owner's TW7 answer).

| Stage | What happens | Who does the work |
|---|---|---|
| Initial call | Demo; alignment on data privacy, processes, integrations, and who signs | Owner |
| Deep-dive with process owners | Map their data sources to the canonical contract; take file exports; agree the first decision to test | Owner, with the product doing the computing |
| Integration pack and pilot timeline | Which sources in what order (files first, then automated feeds); pilot scope and dates; commitment agreed | Owner and their IT |
| Pilot and monitoring | Their plan-against-actual breakdown, options against doing nothing, the first change proved | The product, owner overseeing |
| SaaS | Subscription | — |

D15 still fits: the first change is agreed at the end of the deep-dive rather than at the close of a diagnostic.

Effect on documents: D1 replaced by this path with the two safeguards; commercial plan reframed around pilot fee/credit plus SaaS rather than a priced diagnostic; owner analyst load falls (product computes rather than owner transcribing); two-week plan and website offer become "a deep-dive and pilot on your data", not "a paid diagnostic".
