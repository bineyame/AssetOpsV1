# Owner: independent state-of-the-art UX audit of every screen (2026-11-20)

The owner asked:

> aren't these things and more that i have not stated what would have been easily part of a state of the art ui/ux even if i did not know and point out details(such as progressive disclosure, infomration architecture on the ui side..etc)

The coordinator agreed: these are baseline professional practice (NN/g, IBM Carbon, Material, Apple HIG; Linear, Stripe, Ramp), and the owner should not have had to raise them. Causes it named: the mock-up was built as a content spec with UX treated as later polish; reviews checked the brief and the numbers, not the experience against "would a top SaaS company ship this"; the guide's ideas were applied screen by screen as the owner raised them; the coordinator relayed observations instead of leading. Further standard practice not yet properly covered: first use and help (empty states, first-run, inline term definitions); action feedback (confirmations, undo, loading and error states everywhere); search and saved views (filters in the URL, period comparison); notifications and accountability (what waits for me, what changed since I last looked, who did what); permissions shown; export and sharing; perceived speed; accessibility and phone on every page. Proposed: an independent expert UX evaluation of every screen against the full standard before anything reaches the owner, and one audit of all screens now, feeding the Architect's queue as work, not a document for the owner.

The owner replied, verbatim:

> yes, start the audit
